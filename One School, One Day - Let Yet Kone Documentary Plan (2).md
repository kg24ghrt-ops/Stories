1. THE IDEA
##############################################################################
One school, one day. The film follows the clock: an ordinary morning, the
attack in the afternoon, the soldiers leaving in the evening, the empty
village at night. The coup, the CDM, the NUG and the earthquake are not
explained. They are context in ONE line at the top and ONE line at the end.

  the same township, another school was bombed. The UN said twenty students

2. IMPORTANT CORRECTION: IT WAS AN AFTERNOON ATTACK
##############################################################################
You wanted to open in the morning. Good: the film still can. But the attack
was NOT in the morning. Myanmar Witness (an investigative NGO), The Irrawaddy
and RFA all place it in the afternoon, with helicopters arriving around 1 pm.
A "9:40 am during exams" claim exists in one advocacy letter; no credible
outlet supports it, so do not use it.

So the structure is: MORNING (ordinary school day) -> AFTERNOON (attack) ->
EVENING (soldiers leave with detainees) -> NIGHT (empty school, parents with
no bodies to bury).

5. FULL NARRATION (about 330 words, about 2:50 at a calm pace)
##############################################################################
[Top line on screen, no narration.]

It is a Friday. September 16th, 2022. In Let Yet Kone, a village in
Sagaing, more than two hundred and forty children are in a school inside a
monastery compound. Some are as young as seven. Some of their teachers are
volunteers. The day begins like any other.

Around one in the afternoon, four helicopters arrive. Two land soldiers,
about eighty, residents say. Two open fire on the school. According to
residents and investigators, it goes on for about an hour.

At two o'clock, one parent hears what is happening. By five, the parent
knows their daughter is dead.

At least thirteen people are killed. Reports count at least seven children.
UNICEF says at least eleven.

At half past four, the soldiers leave for another town, taking about fifteen
people with them, The Irrawaddy reported. Seven children. Five teachers.
Three villagers. The villagers are released two days later.

The military says armed rebels were using the school. The villagers deny it.
Investigators found remains of rockets at the school. The United Nations'
investigators say they are collecting evidence.

The next day, an officer tells the parents to come and collect their
children's ashes. The parents refuse. They were never allowed to see their
children's bodies.

[Silence, 4 seconds.]

Night in Let Yet Kone. A school with no children.

[End line on screen and narrated:]
A year later, villagers were working to reopen the school. In May 2025, in
the same township, another school was bombed. The UN said twenty students
and two teachers were killed.

##############################################################################
I cannot download or check footage for you, so treat this as a plan.

What is known to exist (verify and request yourself)
 - Myanmar Witness (Centre for Information Resilience) geolocated images and
   video of the aftermath and published a report. Best source for
   verified-authentic material. Ask them for permission and the correct
   credit line.
   https://myanmarwitness.org/reports/the-tabayin-school-attack
 - The ABC article reproduces Myanmar Witness photos, including
   children's sandals at the school.
   https://www.abc.net.au/news/2022-11-24/myanmar-school-air-strike-junta-helicopter-rockets/101683972
 - Reuters carried a social-media still of the entrance of the school and
   AP/AFP photographers covered the story. These are licensed images: you
   need permission or a licence.
 - The Irrawaddy, RFA, Mizzima, DVB and Myanmar Now published photos and
   video from the village. Each owns its material.

Rules for using footage
 - Use footage only if (a) the owner gives written permission, or (b) your
   competition rules allow credited, short, educational use. Ask your
   organisers which one applies. Always put the source credit on screen.
 - Do NOT use TikTok/Facebook/Telegram clips of the attack as proof unless
   Myanmar Witness or a news organisation has verified them.
 - Do NOT show dead or injured children. Show the place, objects and
   silence. It is more powerful and protects the families.
 - Do not use the faces of the real children or teachers from the village.

If you cannot get permission in time (likely for a school deadline)
 - Make the film from: your own filmed b-roll (labelled "illustration"), a
   map animation, text cards, audio testimony read by a voice actor with a
   credit, and 1-2 licensed or permitted photos. This still works and is
   safer.

Permission request, 3 lines (email to Myanmar Witness or the outlet):
 "Hello, we are students making a non-commercial 5-minute school
  documentary for [competition]. May we use [photo/clip] showing [what] in
  one scene, with the credit '[your credit line]'? We will not edit it other
  than cropping, and we will remove it if you ask."

 Quote rule: paraphrase or use only short phrases (under about 15 words),
 name the outlet, and ask permission for anything longer.

New interviews you can ethically do
 - Do NOT contact survivors, children, or relatives in Myanmar. It can put
   them at risk and re-traumatise them.
 - DO interview an adult Myanmar teacher or educator now living in your area
   or school community who can speak generally about what school means to
   them (not about the attack). Get written consent. Offer to blur the face
   if they have family back home.
 - DO ask one of your own teachers for the closing reflection (10 seconds):
   "What does a classroom mean to you?" This ties it to Teachers' Day.
 - Do not edit any interview so it sounds like they said something
   different.

8. WHAT NOT TO SAY
##############################################################################
 - Do not say the attack was in the morning or during exams.
 - Do not give one exact toll. Say "at least thirteen killed, at least seven
   children (UNICEF: at least eleven)."
 - Do not say who exactly was in the helicopters or name the army unit on
   screen. A residents' claim named a battalion; no official confirmation.
 - Do not say the school was an NUG school. Reports do not establish that.
 - Do not use Wikipedia-only details (for example "20 students taken",
   "50,000 fled", "200 homes torched"). Not confirmed by a Tier 1-2 source.
 - Do not use "massacre" as your own word. You may say that survivors
   and some outlets call it that. Stick to "attack" and "killed."
 - Always include the military's explanation (rebels used the school; bombs
   seized) and that villagers deny it.
 - Do not say the school "reopened"; the source says villagers worked to
   reopen it.
 - Do not say "the junta denied" the Sept 2022 attack. Reports conflict
   (one says it acknowledged helicopter fire, others say it denied).
